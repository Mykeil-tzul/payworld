    The four-party model

    Cardholder

    Myke uses a card issued by his bank to pay for coffee.

    Merchant

    Harbor Café accepts the payment and submits the purchase.

    Acquirer

    The merchant’s acquiring institution connects payment acceptance to the network. A processor can provide technology on its behalf.

    Issuer

    Myke’s issuing bank manages the account and ordinarily approves or declines the authorization.

    Visa is the payment network connecting the acquiring and issuing sides. It routes authorization messages and helps enable clearing and settlement. Visa can also respond on an issuer’s behalf in some circumstances, which this demo leaves outside its scope.

    Authorization, capture, clearing, and settlement

    Authorization asks the issuer whether the purchase can go ahead. Capture tells the acquiring side to submit an approved purchase for processing. Clearing exchanges the transaction details. Settlement moves funds between financial institutions, with merchant funding following the acquiring arrangement.

    Timeouts, retries, refunds, and disputes

    A timeout can hide a decision the issuer already made. The simulator looks up the original result. An operation key suppresses a duplicate command, and the webhook example deduplicates by event ID. A refund is a merchant-initiated credit. A dispute is a separate process typically initiated through the cardholder’s issuer and is outside this simulator.

    Why I built PayWorld

    I’m Myke. After more than a year working at Visa, I wanted to share what I’ve learned about how card payments move through the ecosystem. I picked a vanilla cappuccino because an everyday tap is an easy way to see what each party does.

    Independent educational project using public information. The Visa mark identifies the network in the diagram. PayWorld is unaffiliated as a product and processes no payments, account numbers, or internal data.
